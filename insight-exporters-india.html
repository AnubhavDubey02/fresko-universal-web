What overseas fruit exporters should ask before choosing an India partner

A purchase order is only one part of a perishable-goods trade. The harder question is what happens after the container moves.

1. Who owns the commercial story after arrival?

Ask how the partner records the shipment, separates LOTs, captures quality observations, communicates availability and ties sales back to stock. If those answers live only in scattered chats, visibility becomes difficult as volume grows.

2. How is quality connected to selling?

For perishable fruit, the condition at receipt can affect route-to-market, price and urgency. A useful process makes QC information available to the people making commercial decisions.

3. Can a sale be traced to a LOT?

LOT-level context makes it easier to answer which stock was sold, in what quantity, to whom, and what remains. This becomes especially important when a container contains different condition or pack characteristics.

4. What happens between sale and settlement?

Ask how dispatch and payment are reconciled against deals. A strong commercial relationship benefits when both sides can discuss exceptions from a shared record rather than reconstructing events later.

5. Does the partner understand your intended channel?

Wholesale markets, distribution, hospitality, retail and institutional demand can have different requirements. Discuss the intended route-to-market before treating India as one undifferentiated market.
